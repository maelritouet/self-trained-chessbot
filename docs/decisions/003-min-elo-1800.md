# 003: Keep games where both players are rated at least 1800

- **Date:** 2026-11-09
- **Status:** accepted

## Context
The model imitates the moves in its training data, blunders included. A rating floor trades
game quality against how much data we can collect, and how long collection takes.
Measured on the 20k-game sample from the 2026-10 dump (filtered at 1500): 46.2% of kept games
have both players ≥1800, 18.0% ≥2000, 4.7% ≥2200.

## Options
1. **1500**: most data and fastest to collect (~1 h of streaming for 3M games), but noisier play.
   Leaves the most room for the W5 fine-tune on 2000+ games to show an effect.
2. **1800**: cleaner play for about twice the collection time (~2 h for 3M games).
3. **2000**: strongest games, about 5× slower to collect, and it largely overlaps with the W5
   fine-tuning set, removing most of that experiment's contrast.

## Decision
1800 for both `WhiteElo` and `BlackElo` (`min_elo: 1800`).

## Why
A middle ground: a clearly better standard of play than 1500 while collection time stays
reasonable (a couple of hours, once), and there is still a gap to the 2000+ fine-tuning data.

## Consequences
- Collection is ~2× slower than at 1500 for the same number of games.
- The W5 before/after fine-tuning contrast (1800+ base → 2000+ fine-tune) is narrower than it
  would be from a 1500 base. Note this when reporting that result.
- The speed mix (see 004) and game-length stats (see 006) were measured at 1500; re-check them on
  the 1800 dataset.

## Follow-up (2026-11-09)
Measured on 1.79M games collected at the 1800 floor: median of the weaker player's rating 1955,
90th percentile 2216; 39.5% of games have both players at 2000+. Collection ran at ~450–500 kept
games/s (about 2 h for 3M), as expected. The 2000+ subset is large (~1.2M of 3M games), so W5 has
plenty of fine-tuning data, but the base model already sees much 2000+ play, which narrows the
before/after contrast noted above.
